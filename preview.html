Debits and Credits

Debit means left; credit means right. They describe where an entry goes — not whether it is good or bad.

1 Explanation

The terms debit (Dr.) and credit (Cr.) mean left and right, respectively. They do not mean increase or decrease — they describe where a company makes an entry.

  • An entry on the left side of an account is a debit to the account.
  • An entry on the right side is a credit to the account.
  • An account shows a debit balance if total debits exceed the credits.
  • An account shows a credit balance if total credits exceed the debits.
Debits on the left and credits on the right is simply an accounting custom — like driving on the right-hand side of the road. The custom is now the rule, and it applies to all accounts.

      Debit & Credit Rules

      Each account type increases on one specific side. That side is its normal balance.

      1 Explanation

      Increases to all asset and expense accounts occur on the left (debit) side; decreases on the right (credit) side.

      Conversely, increases to all liability, revenue, and most stockholders' equity accounts occur on the right (credit) side; decreases on the left.

      An account's normal balance is the side that increases it. One exception in the equity family: Dividends increases on the debit side.

      The Double-Entry System

      Every transaction is recorded with equal debits and credits — which lets the books prove themselves.

      1 Explanation

      Under the double-entry system, a company records the dual (two-sided) effect of each transaction in appropriate accounts.

      • This system offers a means of proving the accuracy of the recorded amounts.
      • If every transaction is recorded with equal debits and credits, then the sum of all debits must equal the sum of all credits.

      The Basic Equation

      Because every debit has a matching credit, what a company owns always equals the claims against it.

      1 Explanation

      In a double-entry system, for every debit there must be a credit, and vice versa. This leads to the basic accounting equation:

      Assets = Liabilities + Stockholders' Equity. The left side lists what the company owns; the right side shows the claims against those assets — first creditors, then owners.

      Assets carry debit balances; liabilities and equity carry credit balances. So the equation is really just total debits = total credits, written another way.

      The Expanded Equation

      Stockholders' equity is built from five accounts — three raise it, two lower it.

      1 Explanation

      Illustration 2.3 expands the equation to show the accounts that make up stockholders' equity, with each account's debit/credit rule:

      Assets = Liabilities + Common Stock + Retained Earnings + Revenues − Expenses − Dividends

      Common Stock, Retained Earnings, and Revenues increase equity (credit balances). Expenses and Dividends decrease equity — so they carry debit balances even though they sit under equity.
