Chapter 2 · The Accounting Information System

Debits and Credits

Lecture 1
Lesson 1 · Concept

Debit means left. Credit means right.

The terms debit (Dr.) and credit (Cr.) mean left and right. They do not mean increase or decrease — they describe where a company makes entries in the recording process.

  • An entry on the left side of an account is a debit to the account.
  • An entry on the right side is a credit to the account.
  • An account shows a debit balance if total debits exceed total credits.
  • An account shows a credit balance if total credits exceed total debits.

Under the universally used double-entry accounting system, every transaction is recorded with equal debits and credits — so the sum of all debits must always equal the sum of all credits. That equality is a built-in check on the accuracy of the records. Positioning debits on the left and credits on the right is simply the custom — like driving on the right-hand side of the road, the rule applies to all accounts.

Chapter 2 · Lecture 3

Financial Statements & Ownership Structure

Lecture 3
Lesson · Concept

How the statements relate

The stockholders’ equity section of the balance sheet reports common stock and retained earnings. The income statement reports revenues and expenses. The retained earnings statement reports net income or loss and dividends.

Because a company transfers dividends, revenues, and expenses to retained earnings at the end of the period, a change in any one of these three items affects stockholders’ equity.

Lesson · Ownership Structure

The accounts depend on how the company is owned

The company’s ownership structure dictates the types of accounts that are part of, or affect, the stockholders’ equity section.

Corporation

Commonly uses Common Stock, Paid-in Capital in Excess of Par, and Retained Earnings accounts.

Proprietorship / Partnership

Uses an Owner’s Capital account for the owner’s investment and an Owner’s Drawings account to track withdrawals.
